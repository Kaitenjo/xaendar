Expression syntax

Interpolations, bindings, conditions and loops all take an expression: one TypeScript expression, which reads the public members of the component by name. Expressions can compute and combine values, but not change anything or declare functions: logic with steps belongs in the class.

What an expression can do

Operators, the ternary, optional chaining and nullish coalescing, calls to methods of the component and of the values, indexes, typeof, array and object literals, spread, and the globals of the browser such as Math and JSON. An object literal needs parentheses around it: without them the interpolation renders nothing.

What it cannot do

Assignments, new, functions, this, type assertions, regular expressions and more than one expression are rejected when the template is compiled:

Each of these has a place in the class: a method that assigns, a computed signal that maps a list, a member holding the result of new or of a regular expression.

What it can read

Expressions are type-checked as code outside the class, so they see its public members and inherited ones, without this, plus the globals. Constants of the module are not members, and members marked private or protected are out of reach: expose what the template needs as public members. The checks use the options of your tsconfig.json, strict ones included.

Edge cases

A member named history or location is hidden by the global of the browser with the same name: the template shows window.history and the address of the page. Other names of globals, such as name or status, are not affected. Rename the members. This is listed in Known issues.

The template is checked with the strict options of the project. With noUncheckedIndexedAccess, a property of a Record may be undefined; a readonly member has the literal type of its value, so comparing it with another literal is flagged. The messages show the member with a root. prefix.

Every signal an expression reads is tracked in text, conditions and loops. In an attribute, only the signal members the expression names are: see Binding. Event handlers follow their own, more limited syntax: see Event listeners.
